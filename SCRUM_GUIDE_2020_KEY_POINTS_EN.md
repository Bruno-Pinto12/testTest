# 📚 SCRUM GUIDE 2020 - COMPREHENSIVE STUDY SHEET

**Created for:** PSM I Exam Preparation  
**Based on:** Official Scrum Guide November 2020  
**Language:** English  
**Duration:** 45-60 minutes to study thoroughly

## 🎯 DEFINITION OF SCRUM

> **Scrum is a lightweight framework for developing, delivering, and sustaining complex products through empiricism, accountability, and continuous improvement.**

### Key Principles
- **Lightweight:** Minimal rules and structure
- **Framework:** Not a methodology or complete process
- **Adaptive:** Responds to change and feedback
- **Empirical:** Based on observation and experience
- **Complex problems:** Designed for adaptive solutions to complex challenges

---

## 🏛️ THREE PILLARS OF SCRUM (EMPIRICISM)

Scrum is built on empirical process control with three pillars:

### 1. **TRANSPARENCY**
- All aspects of work must be visible to those responsible for it
- Work, processes, and progress must be clearly visible
- Common understanding of terminology across the team
- DoD (Definition of Done) provides transparency about quality
- **Key point:** Decisions must be based on what is observed, not speculation

### 2. **INSPECTION**
- Scrum artifacts and progress toward the Sprint Goal must be inspected frequently
- Frequent inspection enables adaptation
- **Inspection is NOT the purpose—adaptation is** (common misconception)
- Five events support inspection:
  - Sprint Planning (inspect backlog, forecast capacity)
  - Daily Scrum (inspect progress toward Sprint Goal)
  - Sprint Review (inspect the Increment)
  - Sprint Retrospective (inspect process effectiveness)
  - Product Backlog Refinement (inspect item clarity)

### 3. **ADAPTATION**
- When any aspect of a process deviates from acceptable limits, it must be adjusted
- The adjustment must be made ASAP to minimize further deviation
- Adaptation happens at:
  - Individual level (Developer adjusts approach)
  - Team level (Sprint Backlog adapted in Daily Scrum)
  - Product level (Product Backlog adapted in Sprint Review)
  - Process level (Team process adapted in Retrospective)

## ⚡ FIVE SCRUM VALUES (CRITICAL FOR EXAM)

**COMMITMENT** — Team members commit to the Sprint Goal and to continuous improvement

**FOCUS** — Focus on the Sprint Goal and Product Goal, don't get distracted by external demands

**OPENNESS** — Open about challenges, impediments, and opportunities; open to new ideas and perspectives; willing to learn and change

**RESPECT** — Respect team members' capabilities and independence; respect stakeholders and customers; value different perspectives

**COURAGE** — Courage to make difficult decisions; courage to raise impediments; courage to do the right thing, even when uncomfortable

### How Values Enable Pillars
- **Commitment + Focus** → Enable Transparency (clear priorities)
- **Openness + Respect** → Enable Inspection (psychological safety to speak up)
- **Courage** → Enables Adaptation (willing to change when needed)

### ⚠️ CRITICAL NOTE
**"Earned Value" and "Economic Value Added" are NOT Scrum Values.** These appear in some exam questions but are incorrect. Only these 5 official values exist: Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, Courage.

## 👥 THREE SCRUM ROLES

### Key Principle: No Hierarchy
- The Scrum Team is small, cross-functional, and self-managing
- **There is no hierarchy inside the Scrum Team**
- The whole Scrum Team is accountable for creating a valuable, useful Increment

---

### 1. **PRODUCT OWNER**

**Primary Accountability:**
- Maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team

**Main Responsibilities:**
- Develops and explicitly communicates the **Product Goal** to the organization
- Creates and maintains the **Product Backlog**
- Ensures the Product Backlog is:
  - Visible and transparent
  - Clear and understandable
  - Ordered to best achieve goals and missions
- Ensures Developers understand Product Backlog items to the level needed

**Key Points About Product Owner:**
- ONE person (not a committee, though can be represented)
- Accountable for ordering the backlog to maximize value
- Not a project manager or boss
- Does NOT assign tasks to Developers
- Interacts with Stakeholders, Developers, and Scrum Master
- Can have input from stakeholders, but PO makes final decisions

**Participation in Events:**
- **Sprint Planning:** Participates fully (present entire time)
- **Sprint Review:** Participates fully (critical for feedback)
- **Sprint Retrospective:** Participates as a Scrum Team member
- **Daily Scrum:** May attend ONLY if actively working on Sprint Backlog items as a Developer
- **Product Backlog Refinement:** Works with Developers to refine items (ongoing, not a formal event)

---

### 2. **DEVELOPERS**

**Primary Accountability:**
- Creating a **usable Increment** every Sprint that meets the Definition of Done
- Planning the work necessary to create the Increment

**Main Responsibilities:**
- Self-managing: Decide HOW to do the work (not WHAT—that's the Product Owner's job)
- Creating the Sprint Backlog
- Selecting Product Backlog items for the Sprint
- Sizing Product Backlog items (with input from PO on requirements)
- Collaborating to plan the work
- Ensuring quality by adhering to the **Definition of Done**
- Inspecting progress toward the Sprint Goal during the Daily Scrum
- Adapting the Sprint Backlog as they learn
- Cross-functional: Have all the skills needed to create an Increment

**Key Points About Developers:**
- Self-managing and cross-functional
- **ALL Developers share accountability for Sprint Backlog items** (no individual accountability)
- Developers include anyone with skills needed (testers, designers, architects, etc.)
- **Scrum has NO "tester" role**—testing is part of Development work
- Recommended size: **10 or fewer**
- Are NOT assigned tasks by the Product Owner or Scrum Master
- Are NOT a sub-team of the Product Owner

**Participation in Events:**
- **Sprint Planning:** Essential (plan the work)
- **Daily Scrum:** Required (for Developers; others optional)
- **Sprint Review:** Present completed work
- **Sprint Retrospective:** Essential (improve team process)

---

### 3. **SCRUM MASTER**

**Primary Accountability:**
- Establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide
- Helping everyone understand Scrum theory and practice

**What Scrum Master IS:**
- A servant leader
- A coach and facilitator
- Someone who removes impediments
- Someone who helps the organization adopt Scrum

**What Scrum Master IS NOT:**
- A manager of the Scrum Team
- A command-and-control leader
- A project coordinator
- Someone who assigns tasks
- Someone who monitors individual productivity
- Someone who must attend every event

---

#### **How Scrum Master Serves the Scrum Team:**
- Coaching the team in self-management and cross-functionality
- Helping the team focus on creating high-value Increments
- Removing impediments to the team's progress
- Ensuring Scrum events take place and are positive, productive, and kept within the timebox
- Helping the team understand the need for clear and concise Product Backlog items

#### **How Scrum Master Serves the Product Owner:**
- Finding techniques for effective Product Goal definition and Product Backlog management
- Helping the Scrum Team understand the need for clear and concise Product Backlog items
- Facilitating stakeholder collaboration as requested or needed
- Helping the Product Owner with backlog management techniques

#### **How Scrum Master Serves the Organization:**
- Leading, training, and coaching the organization in Scrum adoption
- Planning and advising Scrum implementations within the organization
- Helping employees and stakeholders understand and enact Scrum
- Removing barriers between stakeholders and Scrum Teams
- Promoting organizational adoption of Scrum

---

**Participation in Events:**
- **Sprint Planning:** Can participate; ensures timebox is respected, doesn't lead it
- **Daily Scrum:** May attend if needed to coach Developers about timebox, but is NOT required
- **Sprint Review:** Participates; facilitates collaboration
- **Sprint Retrospective:** Participates; facilitates as requested/needed

## 📅 SCRUM EVENTS (TIMEBOXED)

All events are timeboxed and create a regular rhythm. Timeboxes are **maximum amounts of time**—events can end early.

### THE SPRINT (Container Event)

**Definition:**
- A fixed-length event of **one month or less** (typically 1-4 weeks)
- The container for all other Scrum events

**Purpose:**
- Create a Done, usable Increment toward the Product Goal
- Achieve a Sprint Goal
- Inspect and adapt toward the Product Goal

**Sprint Rules:**
- All Sprints are the same length (consistency)
- **New Sprint starts immediately after the previous one ends** (NO break between Sprints)
- Changes that endanger the Sprint Goal should be negotiated with the Product Owner
- **Quality focus is maintained** throughout the Sprint

**When to Cancel a Sprint:**
- **ONLY the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint**
- Sprint is cancelled ONLY if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete
- This is **rare** because Sprints are short

**When NOT to cancel:**
- Not everything will be finished (normal—work goes back to Product Backlog)
- External circumstances change (negotiate with PO, but usually continue)
- Velocity was wrong (continue, learn, adapt next Sprint)

---

### SPRINT PLANNING

**Timebox:**
- 8 hours for a 1-month Sprint
- 4 hours for a 2-week Sprint

**Attendees:**
- Scrum Master (facilitates)
- Product Owner (presents items, clarifies value)
- Developers (plan and commit)

**Purpose:**
Sprint Planning initiates the Sprint by answering three questions:

1. **Why is this Sprint valuable?** (Sprint Goal)
2. **What can be Done this Sprint?** (Product Backlog selection)
3. **How will the chosen work get done?** (Sprint Backlog creation)

**Agenda - PART 1: WHY & WHAT (2-4 hours)**
- Product Owner presents top Product Backlog items
- Discussion of value, dependencies, and context
- Developers understand the Sprint Goal
- Consensus on Sprint Goal reached

**Agenda - PART 2: HOW (2-4 hours)**
- Developers plan technical work
- Developers create the Sprint Backlog
- Decompose items into smaller, manageable work
- Estimate effort
- Determine capacity and commitment

**Key Points:**
- **Developers decide HOW MUCH work they can do** (forecast based on capacity and past performance)
- NOT everything must be planned perfectly in detail
- Just enough detail for Developers to understand and forecast
- Developers can adjust and discover more work as they learn
- **If PO and Developers can't agree:**
  - Don't extend Sprint Planning past the timebox
  - Start the Sprint with what IS understood
  - Continue to analyze during the Sprint
  - Discuss clarifications at Sprint Review or in refinement

---

### DAILY SCRUM

**Timebox:**
- **15 minutes** (same for all Sprint lengths)

**Attendees:**
- **Developers: Required**
- Scrum Master: May attend to teach/coach, not required
- Product Owner: May attend if working on Sprint Backlog items, not required

**When:**
- Same time each day (reduces complexity)
- Same place if possible (or same virtual space)
- Every day of the Sprint

**Purpose:**
- Developers inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal
- Developers adapt the Sprint Backlog as needed
- Developers self-organize the next day's work

**Developers discuss:**
- What was completed since the last Daily Scrum?
- What will be completed by the next Daily Scrum?
- What impediments exist?

**What it is NOT:**
- A status report for the Scrum Master or managers
- A detailed technical discussion
- A forum for management oversight

**Key Points:**
- It's an **internal meeting for Developers**
- Focused on progress toward **Sprint Goal** (not individual tasks)
- Identifies impediments (Scrum Master helps remove them)
- Developers self-organize (no tasking from anyone)
- If SM or PO attend: must not disrupt the meeting
- Scrum Master may help Developers keep it within 15 minutes, but doesn't run it

---

### SPRINT REVIEW

**Timebox:**
- 4 hours for a 1-month Sprint
- 2 hours for a 2-week Sprint

**Attendees:**
- Scrum Team (PO, SM, Developers)
- Key stakeholders invited

**Purpose:**
- Inspect the Increment
- Discuss progress toward the Product Goal
- Discuss what to do next based on feedback
- Collaborate on next steps
- Adapt the Product Backlog based on learning

**What Happens:**
- Developers present completed work (ONLY items meeting Definition of Done)
- Work NOT meeting DoD is NOT shown
- Stakeholders provide feedback
- Discussion of market/business changes that affect Product Backlog
- Collaboration on what was learned
- Product Backlog may be re-prioritized based on feedback

**Key Points:**
- It's a **working session**, not a presentation-only meeting
- NOT just a demo (though demo may happen)
- NOT a status report
- Focus on the Increment and collaboration
- Feedback drives Product Backlog changes
- Product Owner updates Product Backlog based on review outcomes

---

### SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE

**Timebox:**
- 1.5 hours (up to 3 hours allowed) for a 1-month Sprint
- 45 minutes for a 2-week Sprint

**Attendees:**
- Scrum Team ONLY: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers
- **Not a forum for external observers or managers**

**Purpose:**
- Inspect how the last Sprint went regarding people, relationships, processes, and tools
- Identify what went well and improvements to make
- Plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness
- Plan how to incorporate lessons into next Sprint

**Scrum Team discusses:**
- What went well during the Sprint?
- What didn't go well during the Sprint?
- What could we improve?
- What will we commit to improve in the next Sprint?

**Key Points:**
- **REQUIRED every Sprint** (cannot be skipped, even if team is busy or says they don't need it)
- Scrum Master facilitates; team is fully engaged
- Focus on process and team effectiveness, NOT on blaming individuals
- Outcomes are **concrete ACTIONS**, not just discussion
- Actions are added to the Sprint Backlog if team chooses to work on them
- Team inspects and adapts how it works together

## 📦 THREE SCRUM ARTIFACTS

All artifacts are designed to maximize transparency of key information.

### 1. **PRODUCT BACKLOG**

**Definition:**
- An emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product
- The single source of work for the Scrum Team

**Characteristics:**
- **Ordered:** By value (Product Owner responsibility) to maximize ROI
- **Dynamic:** Changes as business and market evolve
- **Never complete:** Grows, evolves, and refines over time
- **Belongs to one Product Owner** (one person accountable)
- **For one product:** Multiple teams on same product = ONE Product Backlog

**Items in Product Backlog:**
- User Stories
- Bug fixes
- Non-functional requirements (performance, security, etc.)
- Technical improvements and refactoring
- Infrastructure changes
- Anything that the team might work on

**Product Backlog Management:**
- Product Owner is accountable for managing it
- Product Owner can delegate refinement work but remains accountable
- Developers have input on technical feasibility and sizing
- Can be rearranged, split, added, or removed at any time

**Product Backlog Refinement:**
- Ongoing activity (not a formal event, though can be time-boxed)
- Breaking down items into smaller, estimable pieces
- Adding detail, estimates, and clarification
- Typically 10% of Sprint capacity used for refinement
- PO and Developers collaborate

**Estimation:**
- Developers estimate (with Product Owner providing context)
- Relative sizing (story points, planning poker, etc.) is common
- NOT required by Scrum, but helpful for forecasting
- Estimation is Developers' responsibility

---

### 2. **SPRINT BACKLOG**

**Definition:**
- Items selected from the Product Backlog for the Sprint
- A plan for delivering the Increment
- Created during Sprint Planning

**Composition:**
- The Sprint Goal (why the Sprint is valuable)
- Selected Product Backlog items (what will be done)
- Actionable plan for delivering the Increment (how it will be done)

**Who Owns It:**
- **Developers own the Sprint Backlog**
- Only Developers can change it (remove, add, or update work)
- Product Owner doesn't assign or modify it
- Scrum Master doesn't assign or modify it

**What Can Be Included:**
- User Stories
- Tasks
- Use Cases
- Tests
- Bug fixes
- Anything that's a decomposition of selected Product Backlog items

**Management:**
- Developers add work as they discover it during Sprint
- Work is added **AS SOON AS POSSIBLE** (not just during Daily Scrum)
- Updated continuously during the Sprint
- Visible to all team members
- Developers adjust as they learn and discover dependencies

**Visibility:**
- Sprint Backlog is visible to all
- Usually shown on a Scrum Board (physical or digital)
- Shows what work is to-do, in-progress, and done

**After Sprint:**
- Sprint Backlog exists ONLY for that Sprint
- At end of Sprint, focus shifts to the Increment
- New Sprint = New Sprint Backlog

---

### 3. **INCREMENT**

**Definition:**
- The sum of all Product Backlog items completed during a Sprint
- Plus all the value of Increments from all previous Sprints
- A concrete step toward the Product Goal

**Requirements:**
- **Must be usable** (can be used, tested, and potentially released)
- **Must meet the Definition of Done** (quality standards)
- **Must be presented at Sprint Review** (for stakeholder feedback)

**Multiple Increments:**
- More than one Increment may be created during a Sprint
- All must meet the Definition of Done
- They accumulate across Sprints

**Release:**
- Does NOT have to be released at the end of every Sprint
- **CAN be released when the business decides it makes sense**
- Multiple Sprints' Increments can be combined before release
- Release is a business decision, not a Scrum requirement

**Quality Standards:**
- An Increment with "minor known bugs" is NOT an Increment (it doesn't meet DoD)
- Technical documentation, code reviews, testing, security review are part of creating Increment
- No shortcuts on Definition of Done

## ✅ DEFINITION OF DONE

**What it is:**
- A formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets quality standards
- A shared understanding of what "complete" means
- A checklist of criteria that work must meet

**Purposes (3 critical purposes):**
1. **Create transparency** of the quality and status of work
2. **Create shared understanding** among all team members about what "done" means
3. **Enable team to assess** when work is truly complete

**Who Creates It:**
- **Scrum Team** (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers collaborate)
- May include organizational or technical standards as a baseline

**When to Change It:**
- During Sprint Retrospective
- As team matures and learns, they often add MORE stringent criteria
- Never remove criteria (only add or maintain)

**What it Might Include:**
- Code review complete
- Unit tests written and passing
- Integration tests passing
- Documentation updated
- Security review passed
- Performance benchmarks met
- Deployment ready
- Whatever the team and organization define

**Important Points:**
- Definition of Done should be **achievable WITHIN the Sprint**
- If team lacks skills/tools to meet DoD, work with Scrum Master and organization to improve
- When multiple teams work on one product: **SAME Definition of Done for all**
- May have organizational minimum standards + team-specific additions
- All work must meet DoD; nothing is "partially done"
- Work not meeting DoD goes back to Product Backlog (not counted as Increment)

## 🎯 SPRINT GOAL

**Definition:**
- The single objective for the Sprint
- The reason the Sprint is valuable

**Characteristics:**
- Created during Sprint Planning
- Gives Developers focus while allowing flexibility in approach
- Helps team decide what to do if choices or trade-offs arise
- **Single objective** (one clear focus, not multiple goals)

**Purpose:**
- Focuses Developers on the most valuable work
- Provides coherence and unity of purpose
- Helps communicate Sprint value to stakeholders

**Example of Sprint Goal:**
- ✅ "Implement user authentication" (clear, focused)
- ✅ "Enable users to log in and manage profiles" (achievable, valuable)
- ❌ "Work on database, UI, API, testing, documentation" (too many things, no focus)

**Who Sets It:**
- Collaboration between **Product Owner** (value perspective) and **Developers** (feasibility perspective)
- Created during Sprint Planning
- Reflects the top priorities in Product Backlog

**How It's Used:**
- Referenced in Daily Scrum: "Are we still on track toward the Sprint Goal?"
- Referenced in Sprint Review: "Did we achieve the Sprint Goal?"
- Guides decisions during the Sprint: "Does this work support the Sprint Goal?"
- Can change during Sprint, but ONLY if Sprint Goal becomes obsolete (rare)

---

## 🎁 PRODUCT GOAL

**Definition:**
- Long-term objective for the product
- The future state of the product (5-10 year vision)
- Why the product exists

**New in Scrum Guide 2020:**
- Added to clarify the connection between Sprints and longer-term vision

**Examples:**
- "Become the easiest way to book flights online"
- "Reduce customer support tickets by 50% through better self-service"
- "Enable remote teams to collaborate as if they're in the same room"

**Relationship to Sprints:**
- Product Goal is long-term (lasting multiple Sprints or years)
- Each Sprint Goal contributes to the overall Product Goal
- Multiple Sprints progress incrementally toward Product Goal
- Each Increment moves closer to Product Goal

**Who Manages It:**
- **Product Owner** is accountable for the Product Goal
- With input from Developers and stakeholders
- Clear communication to organization

**How It Guides:**
- Influences ordering of Product Backlog (what adds most value toward goal)
- Helps decide what features and work to prioritize
- Helps team understand "why" they're building what they're building
- Provides context for Sprint Goals

## 🔄 PROCESS FLOW - How It All Connects

```
PRODUCT GOAL
(Why does the product exist? Long-term vision)
    ↓
PRODUCT BACKLOG
(What needs to be done? Ordered list by value)
    ↓
SPRINT PLANNING → SPRINT GOAL
(What will we accomplish this Sprint? Single objective)
    ↓
SPRINT BACKLOG
(How will we accomplish it? Actionable plan)
    ↓
DEVELOPMENT WORK
(Daily Scrum - inspect progress, adapt, self-organize)
    ↓
INCREMENT
(What did we create? Must meet Definition of Done)
    ↓
SPRINT REVIEW
(Show stakeholders, gather feedback, discuss next steps)
    ↓
SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE
(How can we improve? What will we change next Sprint?)
    ↓
(Next Sprint starts immediately - repeat cycle)
```

## ⚠️ COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS (PSM I Traps)

| ❌ WRONG | ✅ CORRECT |
|---|---|
| Scrum Master assigns tasks to Developers | Developers self-manage work; Scrum Master removes impediments |
| Daily Scrum is a status report for Scrum Master | Daily Scrum is Developers self-organizing to achieve Sprint Goal |
| Sprint can be extended if not finished | Sprint length is fixed timebox; unfinished work returns to Product Backlog |
| Definition of Done guides how many items to select | DoD ensures quality standards; doesn't determine quantity of work |
| Scrum Master runs the Daily Scrum | Developers run Daily Scrum; SM may facilitate/coach, not required to attend |
| Product Owner must attend Daily Scrum | PO only attends if actively working on Sprint Backlog items as a Developer |
| Burndown charts are required by Scrum | Nice to have for visualization, but not required by Scrum |
| Teams should rotate every Sprint | Team stability is important; minimize changes in composition |
| Sprint is cancelled when work isn't finished | Work is done when it's done; Sprint continues, unfinished work goes to Product Backlog |
| Scrum Master is a project manager | Scrum Master is a servant leader; not a manager or command-and-control leader |
| Product Owner is a boss or manager | Product Owner is part of the team; not a boss; doesn't assign work |
| Testing only happens at the end of Sprint | Testing is continuous throughout Sprint; part of Definition of Done |
| Only Developers can attend Retrospective | Scrum Team (PO, SM, Developers) attends; external observers not included |
| Confusing project language with Scrum | Scrum focuses on products, not projects; Scrum is product-oriented |
| Refinement is a formal event in the Sprint | Refinement is ongoing; not a formal event; uses ~10% of Sprint capacity |

## 🧠 STUDY TIPS FOR PSM I SUCCESS

1. **Memorize the 5 Scrum Values word-for-word:** Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, Courage. These WILL be tested.

2. **Know timeboxes by heart:** Can you recall them without notes? Sprint (≤1 month), Planning (8h), Daily (15m), Review (4h), Retro (1.5h)

3. **Understand roles by accountability, not tasks:**
   - PO = maximize value
   - Developers = create Increment
   - SM = establish Scrum, help everyone learn

4. **Events are KEY—understand PURPOSE, not just agenda:** Each event exists for a reason.

5. **Empiricism is the foundation:** Everything in Scrum is based on Transparency → Inspection → Adaptation

6. **Developers = self-managing:** Not managed, not assigned tasks, not told how to work

7. **Product Owner is ONE person:** Can delegate, but accountable for decisions and backlog ordering

8. **Scrum Master ≠ Project Manager:** Servant leader, facilitator, impediment remover, coach

9. **Multiple teams = ONE Product Backlog, ONE Product Owner, ONE Definition of Done:** This is tested multiple times in different ways

10. **When in doubt:** Go back to the Scrum Guide 2020 definition. The exam tests according to official guide.

## 📚 QUICK REFERENCE - TIMEBOXES CHEAT SHEET

| Event | 1-month Sprint | 2-week Sprint | Fixed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sprint** | 1 month (30 days) | 2 weeks (10 days) | Yes, all same |
| **Sprint Planning** | 8 hours | 4 hours | Max - can end early |
| **Daily Scrum** | 15 minutes | 15 minutes | Max - can end early |
| **Sprint Review** | 4 hours | 2 hours | Max - can end early |
| **Sprint Retrospective** | 1.5 hours (3h max) | 45 minutes | Max - can end early |

**Key point:** Timeboxes are MAXIMUM times. Events should end when their purpose is achieved, not when the timebox expires.

## 🎯 REAL-LIFE CASES FOR PSM I EXAM

### **Case 1: Two Scrum Teams on the same product**

**Question:** Do they share Definition of Done and Product Backlog?

**Answer:**

| Item | Shared? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Product Backlog** | ✅ **YES (one backlog)** | Backlog belongs to the product, not a team. Each team pulls items. Prevents conflicting work. |
| **Product Owner** | ✅ **YES (one PO)** | One person is accountable for maximizing product value and ordering the single backlog |
| **Product Goal** | ✅ **YES** | One product = one long-term objective |
| **Definition of Done** | ✅ **YES (SAME for all teams)** | Scrum Guide: Multiple teams must mutually define and comply with same DoD. One integrated Increment. |
| **Increment** | ✅ **YES (one integrated Increment)** | Each team's work combines into single usable, "Done" Increment |
| **Sprint Backlog** | ❌ NO | Each team owns its own Sprint Backlog |
| **Sprint Goal** | ❌ NO | Each team can have its own Sprint Goal supporting Product Goal |
| **Developers/Scrum Master** | ❌ NO | Each team has own Developers; each team has Scrum Master (may be same person for multiple teams) |

**Why This Matters:**
- Separate Product Backlogs = lost single order of priorities = teams build conflicting things
- Different DoD = Increments don't integrate = combined product not releasable
- If DoD is organizational standard, all teams follow it as MINIMUM (can add stricter criteria, can't drop any)

---

### **Case 2: Product Owner Too Busy for Sprint Planning**

**Question:** What should happen?

**Answer:** Product Owner is part of Scrum Team and MUST participate in Sprint Planning. Their input on value and Sprint Goal is essential. They can:
- Delegate backlog refinement work
- Have other people prep items
- **BUT they remain accountable and must attend Planning**
- Scrum Master helps PO find techniques to manage time

---

### **Case 3: Manager Asks Scrum Master for Status Report on Developers**

**Question:** What should Scrum Master do?

**Answer:** 
- Scrum Master is NOT a manager of Developers
- Developers are self-managing; progress is transparent through Sprint Backlog and Increment
- Scrum Master should help stakeholder understand:
  - Progress is shown in Sprint Review
  - Increment is the measure of progress
  - Developers' focus is Sprint Goal, not individual tasks
  - Invite manager to Sprint Review for transparency

---

### **Case 4: Stakeholder Asks Developers to Add Urgent Work Mid-Sprint**

**Question:** What should happen?

**Answer:**
- Request goes to Product Owner (not to Developers directly)
- PO can clarify work and negotiate scope
- Developers decide if work can fit given Sprint Goal
- Sprint Goal cannot be endangered
- Developers don't take orders directly; they work through PO

---

### **Case 5: Sprint Goal Becomes Obsolete**

**Question:** Who can cancel Sprint? What happens?

**Answer:**
- **ONLY Product Owner can cancel Sprint**
- Sprint is cancelled ONLY if Sprint Goal becomes obsolete
- This is RARE because Sprints are short
- Work-in-progress returns to Product Backlog
- Any completed, Done work becomes Increment

---

### **Case 6: Team Can't Finish Everything by End of Sprint**

**Question:** What should happen?

**Answer:**
- Sprint is NOT extended (timebox is fixed)
- Unfinished work returns to Product Backlog
- Product Owner re-orders it for next Sprint
- Work not meeting Definition of Done is NOT part of Increment
- Team inspects why in Retrospective and adapts capacity forecast

---

### **Case 7: Definition of Done is Not Met for One Item**

**Question:** Is it part of the Increment?

**Answer:**
- ❌ NO, it is NOT an Increment
- Item goes BACK to Product Backlog
- Will be worked on when Product Owner prioritizes it
- Cannot be considered "done" work

---

### **Case 8: Developer Absent or Someone Added Mid-Sprint**

**Question:** How does team handle?

**Answer:**
- Team stays self-managing and adapts Sprint Backlog
- Sprint Goal still guides the work
- Changes in composition can affect focus and productivity
- Team should handle deliberately and communicate with SM

---

### **Case 9: Scrum Master Also Acting as a Developer**

**Question:** Is this allowed?

**Answer:**
- ✅ Yes, this is allowed
- ❌ BUT Scrum Master must not neglect Scrum Master responsibilities
- Role conflict possible; must ensure both roles are fulfilled
- Organization benefits; team capacity increases

---

### **Case 10: Team Wants to Skip Retrospective Because Busy**

**Question:** What should Scrum Master do?

**Answer:**
- Retrospective is a **REQUIRED event** (not optional)
- Cannot be skipped, even if team is busy
- It is how team inspects and adapts how it works together
- Skipping it removes the opportunity for improvement
- Scrum Master facilitates it; team is fully involved

---

### **Case 11: Product Owner Wants to Add Work to Sprint Backlog**

**Question:** Can PO add items during Sprint?

**Answer:**
- Product Owner can PROPOSE items
- Developers OWN Sprint Backlog and DECIDE what they can take on
- Product Owner does NOT assign work
- If added, unfinished work is removed to keep Sprint commitment feasible
- Only Developers modify Sprint Backlog

---

### **Case 12: Multiple Teams with Different Sprint Lengths**

**Question:** Is this allowed?

**Answer:**
- Scrum Guide doesn't explicitly REQUIRE aligned Sprints
- But aligned Sprints are common practice (easier integration)
- For exam: Answer according to what Scrum Guide requires, not common practice
