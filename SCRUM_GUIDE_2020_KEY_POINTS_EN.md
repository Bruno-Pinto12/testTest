# 📚 SCRUM GUIDE 2020 - KEY POINTS STUDY SHEET

**Created for:** PSM I Exam Preparation  
**Based on:** Official Scrum Guide November 2020  
**Language:** English  
**Duration:** 30-45 minutes to study

---

## 🎯 DEFINITION OF SCRUM

> **Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people, teams, and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems.**

### Key Principles
- **Lightweight:** Minimal rules and structure
- **Framework:** Not a methodology or complete process
- **Adaptive:** Responds to change and feedback
- **Empirical:** Based on observation and experience

---

## 🏛️ THREE PILLARS OF SCRUM

### 1. **TRANSPARENCY**
- All aspects of work must be visible to those responsible for it
- Work, processes, and progress must be clearly visible
- Common understanding of terminology
- DoD (Definition of Done) provides transparency

### 2. **INSPECTION**
- Scrum artifacts and progress toward the Sprint Goal must be inspected
- Frequent inspection enables adaptation
- Inspection is not the purpose—adaptation is
- Four events support inspection: Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective, Daily Scrum, Sprint Planning

### 3. **ADAPTATION**
- When any aspect of a process deviates from acceptable limits, it must be adjusted
- The adjustment must be made ASAP to minimize further deviation
- Processes, team behavior, and product all adapt based on inspection

---

## ⚡ FIVE SCRUM VALUES (CRITICAL FOR EXAM)

### 1. **COMMITMENT**
- Team members commit to the Sprint Goal
- Commitment to continuous improvement

### 2. **FOCUS**
- Focus on the Sprint Goal
- Don't get distracted by external demands
- Build Product Backlog items to maximize value

### 3. **OPENNESS**
- Open about challenges and impediments
- Open to new ideas and perspectives
- Willing to learn and change

### 4. **RESPECT**
- Respect team members' capabilities and independence
- Respect stakeholders and customers
- Value different perspectives

### 5. **COURAGE**
- Courage to make difficult decisions
- Courage to raise impediments
- Courage to do the right thing, even when uncomfortable

**⚠️ NOTE:** "Earned Value" and "Economic Value Added" are NOT Scrum Values. Only these 5 are official.

## 👥 THREE SCRUM ROLES

### 1. **PRODUCT OWNER**
**Accountability:**
- Ordering Product Backlog items to maximize value
- Ensuring the Product Backlog is visible and clear
- Ensuring team understands backlog items

**Key Responsibilities:**
- Sole person responsible for Product Backlog management
- Can have input from stakeholders, but PO decides
- Product Owner is ONE PERSON (can be represented, but accountable individually)
- Works with Developers on refinement

**In Events:**
- Attends Sprint Planning to present Product Backlog items
- Attends Sprint Review to collect feedback
- Participates in Daily Scrum only if actively working on Sprint Backlog items (as a Developer)
- NOT required at Sprint Retrospective

---

### 2. **DEVELOPERS**
**Accountability:**
- Creating a plan for the Sprint (Sprint Backlog)
- Ensuring the quality of the Increment
- Adapting their plan as they learn

**Key Responsibilities:**
- Self-managing: decide HOW to do the work
- Selecting Product Backlog items for the Sprint
- Sizing Product Backlog items (with input from PO)
- Creating an Increment that meets Definition of Done
- Organizing and managing their own work

**Important:**
- No individual accountability for Sprint Backlog items
- ALL Developers share accountability for the Sprint Backlog
- Developers include anyone with skills needed (testers, designers, etc.)
- Scrum has NO "tester" role—testing is part of Development work

**Recommended Size:** 10 or fewer (Scrum Guide 2020)

---

### 3. **SCRUM MASTER**
**Accountability:**
- Establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide
- Helping everyone understand Scrum theory and practice

**Key Responsibilities:**
- **Servant Leader:** Not a manager or boss
- Remove impediments that hinder the team
- Facilitate Scrum events (not lead them)
- Coach the team in self-management
- Help organization adopt Scrum

**NOT Responsible For:**
- Assigning tasks
- Monitoring individual productivity
- Leading Daily Scrum (Developers run it)
- Updating Burndown charts
- Updating Sprint Backlog

**In Events:**
- Sprint Planning: Facilitates, ensures timebox is respected
- Daily Scrum: Teaches Developers to keep it within 15 minutes, is NOT required to attend
- Sprint Review: Facilitates but doesn't present the work
- Sprint Retrospective: Facilitates as requested/needed

## 📅 FOUR SCRUM EVENTS (TIMEBOXED)

All events are timeboxed and create a regular rhythm.

### 1. **SPRINT**
- **Duration:** 1 month or less (typically 1-4 weeks)
- **Timebox:** Container for all other events
- **Who decides duration:** Team decides (with PO input)
- **Consistency:** All Sprints are same length

**Key Rules:**
- Starts immediately after previous Sprint ends (NO break)
- New Sprint starts immediately after last Sprint ends
- Can be cancelled ONLY by the Product Owner

**When to cancel:**
- Sprint Goal becomes obsolete
- External circumstances make Sprint Goal irrelevant

**When NOT to cancel:**
- Not everything will be finished (this is normal)
- Just continue and adapt

---

### 2. **SPRINT PLANNING**
- **Timebox:** 8 hours for a 1-month Sprint (4 hours for 2-week)
- **Attendees:** Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developers
- **Purpose:** Plan work for the Sprint

**Agenda (two parts):**

**PART 1 - WHAT (2-4 hours):**
- PO presents top Product Backlog items
- Discuss value and dependencies
- Developers understand Sprint Goal
- Consensus on Sprint Goal reached

**PART 2 - HOW (2-4 hours):**
- Developers plan technical work
- Create Sprint Backlog
- Decompose items into smaller tasks
- Estimate effort

**Key Points:**
- Developers decide HOW much work they can do (forecast)
- NOT everything must be planned perfectly
- Enough detail for Developers to understand and forecast
- Developers can adjust as they learn

**If PO and Developers can't agree:**
- Don't extend Sprint Planning past timebox
- Start Sprint with what's understood
- Continue to analyze during Sprint
- Discuss again at Sprint Review

---

### 3. **DAILY SCRUM**
- **Timebox:** 15 minutes
- **Attendees:** Developers only (required)
- **Who starts:** Developers decide
- **When:** Same time, same place (reduces complexity)
- **Purpose:** Inspect progress toward Sprint Goal

**Developers discuss:**
- What was completed since last Daily Scrum
- What will be completed by next Daily Scrum
- What impediments exist

**NOT a status report for:**
- Scrum Master
- Managers
- Stakeholders

**Product Owner & Scrum Master:**
- Can attend, but if present, must not disrupt
- Scrum Master may need to teach Developers about timebox

**Key Points:**
- Internal meeting for Developers
- Focused on progress toward Sprint Goal
- Identifies impediments
- Developers self-organize next day's work

---

### 4. **SPRINT REVIEW**
- **Timebox:** 4 hours for a 1-month Sprint (2 hours for 2-week)
- **Attendees:** Scrum Team + Stakeholders
- **Purpose:** Inspect the Increment and adapt the Product Backlog

**What happens:**
- Developers present completed work (ONLY items meeting Definition of Done)
- Stakeholders provide feedback
- Discuss: What to do next based on feedback
- Discuss market/business changes that affect Product Backlog

**Key Points:**
- NOT a demo (though demo may happen)
- NOT a status report
- Focus on the Increment and next steps
- Collaboration between team and stakeholders
- Product Backlog may change based on feedback

---

### 5. **SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE** (Not in 4 events, but REQUIRED)
- **Timebox:** 1.5 hours for 1-month Sprint (45 min for 2-week)
- **Attendees:** Scrum Team ONLY (PO, SM, Developers)
- **Purpose:** Improve the team's process and effectiveness

**Team discusses:**
- What went well
- What didn't go well
- What will we improve in next Sprint

**Key Points:**
- EVERY Sprint must have one (even if team wants to skip, SM facilitates it)
- Focus on process, not individuals
- Outcomes are ACTIONS, not just talk
- Team adds actions to Sprint Backlog if they choose

## 📦 THREE SCRUM ARTIFACTS

### 1. **PRODUCT BACKLOG**
**Definition:**
- An emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product
- Single source of truth for product requirements

**Characteristics:**
- **Ordered:** By value (PO responsibility)
- **Dynamic:** Changes as business/market changes
- **Never complete:** Grows and evolves with the product
- **Belongs to one Product Owner**
- **For one product:** Multiple teams = ONE Product Backlog

**Items in Product Backlog:**
- User Stories
- Bug fixes
- Non-functional requirements
- Technical improvements
- Any work needed

**Refinement:**
- Ongoing process (not a formal event)
- PO and Developers work together
- Items broken into smaller, estimable pieces
- Typically 10% of Sprint capacity used for refinement

**Estimation:**
- Developers estimate (with PO input on requirements)
- Relative sizing (story points, planning poker, etc.)
- NOT required by Scrum, but helpful

---

### 2. **SPRINT BACKLOG**
**Definition:**
- Items selected from Product Backlog for Sprint
- Plan to deliver the Increment and achieve Sprint Goal

**Who owns it:** Developers (they manage it)

**Creation:** During Sprint Planning

**What includes:**
- Selected Product Backlog items
- Plan for delivering them
- Sprint Goal

**What can be included:**
- User Stories
- Tasks
- Use Cases
- Tests
- Bug fixes
- Anything else that's a decomposition of selected PBIs

**Management:**
- Developers add work as they discover it
- Work is added AS SOON AS POSSIBLE (not just during Daily Scrum)
- Updated continuously during Sprint
- Visible to all team members

**After Sprint:**
- Sprint Backlog exists only for that Sprint
- New Sprint = New Sprint Backlog

---

### 3. **INCREMENT**
**Definition:**
- Sum of all Product Backlog items completed during Sprint
- Plus the value of all previous Sprints' Increments

**Must:**
- Meet the Definition of Done
- Be usable and valuable
- Be presented at Sprint Review

**Release:**
- Does NOT have to be released every Sprint
- CAN be released when business decides it makes sense
- Multiple Sprints' Increments can be combined before release

**Quality:**
- An Increment with "minor known bugs" is NOT an Increment (fails DoD)
- Technical documentation, code reviews, testing are part of creating Increment

## ✅ DEFINITION OF DONE (CRITICAL)

**What it is:**
- Shared understanding of what it means for work to be "complete"
- Checklist of criteria that must be met

**Purposes:**
1. Create transparency of work quality
2. Create shared understanding among team members
3. Enable team to know when work is truly complete

**Who creates it:**
- Scrum Team (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers)
- May include organizational standards

**When to change:**
- During Sprint Retrospective
- As team matures, they often add MORE stringent criteria

**What it should include:**
- Code review complete
- Tests written and passing
- Documentation updated
- Security review passed
- Performance acceptable
- Whatever the team defines

**Important:**
- Definition of Done should be achievable WITHIN the Sprint
- If team lacks skills/tools, adjust DoD with team input
- May be different if organizational standards exist
- When multiple teams work on one product, they should use the SAME Definition of Done

---

## 🎯 SPRINT GOAL

**What it is:**
- Single objective for the Sprint
- Reason for Sprint's value
- Provides focus and direction

**Purpose:**
- Focuses Developers on most important work
- Helps team decide what to do if choices arise

**Example:**
- "Implement user authentication" (instead of implementing 5 random features)

**Who sets it:**
- Collaboration between PO and Developers during Sprint Planning

**When used:**
- Referenced in Daily Scrum (are we still on track?)
- Referenced in Sprint Review (did we achieve it?)
- Can change during Sprint, but only if Sprint Goal becomes obsolete

---

## 🎁 PRODUCT GOAL

**Definition (NEW in 2020):**
- Long-term objective for the product
- Why the product exists

**Examples:**
- "Become the easiest way to book flights"
- "Reduce customer support tickets by 50%"

**Relationship to Sprints:**
- Product Goal is long-term
- Multiple Sprints progress toward Product Goal
- Each Sprint Goal contributes to overall Product Goal

**Who manages it:**
- Product Owner (with input from Developers and stakeholders)

**How it guides:**
- Influences ordering of Product Backlog
- Helps decide what features to build
- Helps team understand "why" they're building

---

## 🔄 PROCESS FLOW

```
PRODUCT GOAL (Why does the product exist?)
    ↓
PRODUCT BACKLOG (What needs to be done?)
    ↓
SPRINT PLANNING → SPRINT GOAL (What will we accomplish this Sprint?)
    ↓
SPRINT BACKLOG (How will we accomplish it?)
    ↓
(Daily Scrum - inspect progress)
    ↓
DEVELOPMENT WORK
    ↓
INCREMENT (What did we create?)
    ↓
SPRINT REVIEW (Show stakeholders, gather feedback)
    ↓
SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE (How can we improve?)
    ↓
(Repeat with next Sprint)
```

## ⚠️ COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

### ❌ WRONG → ✅ CORRECT

| Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|
| Scrum Master assigns tasks | Developers self-manage work |
| Daily Scrum is status report | Daily Scrum is self-organization |
| Sprint can be extended | Sprint length is fixed |
| Release every Sprint required | Release when business decides |
| Definition of Done guides how many items to select | DoD ensures quality, not quantity |
| Scrum Master runs the team | Scrum Master serves the team |
| Product Owner must attend Daily Scrum | PO only attends if working on tasks |
| Burndown charts are required | Nice to have, not required |
| Teams can rotate every Sprint | Team stability is important |
| Sprint is cancelled when work isn't finished | Sprint continues, team adapts |

## 🔑 KEY TERMS FOR EXAM

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Timebox** | Maximum amount of time allowed for event; stop when timebox ends |
| **Definition of Done** | Shared criteria for when work is complete |
| **Increment** | Working product that meets Definition of Done |
| **Product Backlog** | Ordered list of everything needed for the product |
| **Sprint Backlog** | Items selected from Product Backlog for current Sprint |
| **Sprint Goal** | Single objective for the Sprint |
| **Product Goal** | Long-term objective for the product |
| **Empiricism** | Decisions based on observation and experimentation |
| **Transparency** | Visibility of work, process, and progress |
| **Inspection** | Regular examination of artifacts and progress |
| **Adaptation** | Adjusting process based on inspection |
| **Self-Managing** | Team decides HOW to do work, not WHAT to do |

## 🧠 STUDY TIPS FOR PSM I

1. **Memorize the 5 Scrum Values:** They will definitely be tested
2. **Know timeboxes by heart:** Can you recall them without notes?
3. **Understand roles:** Each role is defined by accountability, not tasks
4. **Events are KEY:** Understand PURPOSE, not just agenda
5. **Empiricism is important:** Transparency → Inspection → Adaptation cycle
6. **Developers = self-managing:** Not managed, not assigned tasks
7. **Product Owner is single person:** Can delegate, but accountable for decisions
8. **Scrum Master ≠ Project Manager:** Facilitator, not manager
