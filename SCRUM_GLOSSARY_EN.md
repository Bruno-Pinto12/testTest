# Scrum Glossary (English)

This glossary is a study aid based on the November 2020 Scrum Guide; the official
Guide is authoritative if a definition here is unclear or incomplete.

## Foundations and principles

| Term | Concise definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Scrum** | A lightweight framework that helps people, teams, and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems. |
| **Product** | A vehicle to deliver value, with a clear boundary, known stakeholders, and well-defined users or customers. |
| **Value** | The benefit, worth, or usefulness of something. |
| **Empiricism** | The idea that knowledge comes from experience and decisions are based on what is observed. |
| **Empirical process control** | Managing work through transparency, inspection, and adaptation. |
| **Lean thinking** | An approach that reduces waste and focuses on what is essential. |
| **Transparency** | Making important aspects of the work and process visible and understood by those responsible for the work and those receiving it. |
| **Inspection** | Frequent, careful checking of Scrum artifacts and progress toward agreed goals to detect problems or unwanted differences. |
| **Adaptation** | Adjusting a process or product as soon as possible when inspection shows that it is outside acceptable limits. |

## Scrum values

| Term | Concise definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Commitment** | The Scrum Team’s commitment to achieving its goals and supporting one another. |
| **Focus** | Concentrating on the work of the Sprint and the goals of the Scrum Team. |
| **Openness** | Being open about the work and challenges with the Scrum Team and stakeholders. |
| **Respect** | Recognizing one another as capable, independent people. |
| **Courage** | Having the courage to do the right thing and work on tough problems. |

## Scrum Team and accountabilities

| Term | Concise definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Scrum Team** | One Product Owner, one Scrum Master, and Developers: a cohesive, cross-functional, self-managing team focused on the Product Goal, with no sub-teams or internal hierarchies. |
| **Accountabilities** | Scrum defines three accountabilities within the Scrum Team: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. |
| **Product Owner** | The accountability for maximizing product value and managing the Product Backlog effectively. |
| **Scrum Master** | The accountability for establishing Scrum as defined in the Guide and for the Scrum Team’s effectiveness. |
| **Developers** | The people in the Scrum Team committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint. |
| **Cross-functional** | Having, as a team, all the skills needed to create value in each Sprint. |
| **Self-managing** | Deciding internally who does what, when, and how. |
| **Stakeholder** | A person outside the Scrum Team with an interest in the product or its outcome. |
| **Impediment** | An obstacle to progress that the Scrum Master may help the Scrum Team remove. |

## Scrum events

| Term | Concise definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Event** | A formal opportunity in Scrum to inspect and adapt; events enable transparency and provide regularity. |
| **Sprint** | A fixed-length event of one month or less that contains the other Scrum events and turns ideas into value. |
| **Sprint Planning** | The event where the Scrum Team plans why the Sprint is valuable, what can be done, and how the chosen work will get done. |
| **Daily Scrum** | A 15-minute event for Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan for the next workday. |
| **Sprint Review** | The event where the Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the Sprint’s outcome and determine future adaptations. |
| **Sprint Retrospective** | The event where the Scrum Team plans ways to increase quality and effectiveness. |
| **Sprint cancellation** | Only the Product Owner may cancel a Sprint, and only when its Sprint Goal becomes obsolete. |
| **Timebox** | The maximum duration set for an event: for a one-month Sprint, Planning is at most eight hours, the Daily Scrum 15 minutes, Review four hours, and Retrospective three hours. |
| **Forecast** | The Developers’ prediction of the work they believe can be completed in a Sprint; it is not a promise. |

## Artifacts and their commitments

Scrum has three artifacts, each with a commitment that focuses progress and
inspection. Here, “commitment” means the focus associated with an artifact; it
is distinct from **Commitment**, one of the five Scrum values.

| Term | Concise definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Artifact** | A formal description of work or value, designed to make key information transparent. |
| **Product Backlog** | The emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product; it is the single source of work for the Scrum Team. |
| **Product Goal** | A future state of the product that serves as the Scrum Team’s long-term objective and commitment for the Product Backlog. |
| **Product Backlog item** | An item in the Product Backlog that is refined as needed; its description, order, and size may evolve. |
| **Refinement** | The ongoing work of breaking down and further defining Product Backlog items by adding details, such as description, order, and size. |
| **Sprint Backlog** | The Sprint Goal, the Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint, and the Developers’ plan for delivering the Increment. |
| **Sprint Goal** | The single objective for the Sprint and commitment for the Sprint Backlog. |
| **Increment** | A concrete, usable step toward the Product Goal that meets the Definition of Done, adds to previous Increments, and works with them. |
| **Definition of Done** | The formal description of the state of an Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product; it is the commitment for the Increment. |
| **Done** | The state of an Increment that meets the Definition of Done; work that does not meet it is not part of the Increment. |

The artifact commitments are **Product Goal** for the Product Backlog,
**Sprint Goal** for the Sprint Backlog, and **Definition of Done** for the
Increment.

Official source: [The Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html).
