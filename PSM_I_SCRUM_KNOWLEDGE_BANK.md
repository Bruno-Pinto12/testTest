## Question 1 Feedback

**Topic:** Product Increment Release Requirements

**Key Concept:** The product Increment should be usable at the end of every Sprint, but it does NOT have to be released to production.

**Related Concepts:**
- Definition of Done
- Increment definition
- Product release strategy

## Question 2 Feedback

**Topic:** Developer Team Composition and Changes

**Key Concept:** Developers should be replaced AS NEEDED while accounting for short-term productivity reduction.

**Related Concepts:**
- Team stability and cohesion
- Team development stages (Tuckman: Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing)
- Cross-functionality

---

## Question 3 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Review Purpose and Nature

**Key Concept:** The Sprint Review is when the Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the outcome of a Sprint and figure out what to do next.

**Related Concepts:**
- Inspection and Adaptation
- Stakeholder collaboration
- Product Backlog refinement
- Sprint events

---

## Question 4 Feedback

**Topic:** Scrum Team Composition

**Key Concept:** The Scrum Team consists of ONE Product Owner, ONE Scrum Master, and Developers (typically 10 or fewer).

**Related Concepts:**
- Scrum roles and accountabilities
- Cross-functional teams
- Team cohesion

---

## Question 5 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Goal Protection and Change Management

**Key Concept:** Items selected for a Sprint serve the Sprint Goal. No changes should be made that endanger the Sprint Goal. No one external to the Scrum Team can force changes on the Developers.

**Related Concepts:**
- Sprint Goal protection
- Developer self-management
- Product Owner as sole authority on Product Backlog
- Change management during Sprint

---

## Question 6 Feedback

**Topic:** Daily Scrum Attendance Requirements

**Key Concept:** Only the Developers are REQUIRED to attend the Daily Scrum. If the Product Owner or Scrum Master are actively working on Sprint Backlog items, they participate as Developers.

**Related Concepts:**
- Daily Scrum purpose
- Developer accountability
- Self-management
- Role flexibility in Scrum

---

## Question 7 Feedback

**Topic:** Management Role in Scrum

**Key Concept:** Management has no official role on a Scrum Team, but external management is relevant in setting vision and strategy.

**Related Concepts:**
- Scrum Master role
- Product Owner role
- Organizational change
- Self-managing teams

---

## Question 8 Feedback

**Topic:** Scrum Master Role in Daily Scrum

**Key Concept:** The Scrum Master teaches and facilitates, but the Developers are responsible for conducting the Daily Scrum.

**Related Concepts:**
- Scrum Master as servant leader and facilitator
- Developer self-management
- Timeboxes
- Daily Scrum purpose

---

## Question 9 Feedback

**Topic:** Empiricism and Scrum Pillars

**Key Concept:** The three pillars of Scrum are Transparency, Inspection, and Adaptation.

**Related Concepts:**
- Empiricism
- Transparency, Inspection, Adaptation cycle
- Scrum values and how they enable pillars

---

## Question 10 Feedback

**Topic:** Developer Skills and Responsibilities

**Key Concept:** Developers are cross-functional and have all the skills needed to turn selected Product Backlog items into an Increment of useful and valuable product.

**Related Concepts:**
- Cross-functional teams
- Definition of Done
- Developer accountability
- Increment quality

---

## Question 11 Feedback

**Topic:** Timebox Definition

**Key Concept:** A timeboxed event has a MAXIMUM amount of time allowed. Events can end early.

**Related Concepts:**
- All Scrum events are timeboxed
- Event timeboxes: Planning (8h), Daily (15m), Review (4h), Retrospective (1.5h)
- Sprint as a timebox

---

## Question 12 Feedback

**Topic:** Definition of Done Ownership

**Key Concept:** If Definition of Done is not an organizational standard, the Scrum Team must create one appropriate for the product.

**Related Concepts:**
- Definition of Done purposes
- Multiple teams and shared DoD
- Quality standards

---

## Question 13 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Review Timebox

**Key Concept:** Sprint Review is timeboxed to a maximum of 4 hours for a one-month Sprint. For shorter Sprints, it's usually shorter.

**Related Concepts:**
- Timeboxes
- Sprint Review purpose
- Event scaling for different Sprint lengths

---

## Question 14 Feedback

**Topic:** Multiple Teams and Definition of Done

**Key Concept:** When multiple Scrum Teams work on a single product, all teams must have a Definition of Done that makes their combined Increment valuable and useful.

**Related Concepts:**
- Scaling Scrum
- Multiple teams on one product
- Quality consistency
- Increment integration

---

## Question 15 Feedback

**Topic:** Work Progress Management During Sprint

**Key Concept:** The Developers are responsible for managing the progress of work during a Sprint.

**Related Concepts:**
- Developer self-management
- Daily Scrum
- Sprint Backlog
- Progress visibility

---

## Question 16 Feedback

**Topic:** Developer Accountability

**Key Concept:** The entire Scrum Team is accountable for creating value every Sprint. No individual Developer is solely accountable for a Product Backlog item.

**Related Concepts:**
- Scrum Team accountability
- Collective ownership
- Cross-functional collaboration
- No individual task assignments

---

## Question 17 Feedback

**Topic:** Scrum Master Service and Enablement

**Key Concept:** Scrum Master serves by facilitating Developer decision-making and removing impediments.

**Related Concepts:**
- Scrum Master role
- Servant leadership
- Impediment removal
- Facilitation vs. command-and-control

---

## Question 18 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Conclusion

**Key Concept:** A Sprint concludes when the Sprint Retrospective is complete. A Sprint can be cancelled early only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.

**Related Concepts:**
- Sprint as a container
- Scrum events order
- Sprint conclusion
- Sprint cancellation

---

## Question 19 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Length Determination

**Key Concept:** Sprint length should consider business risk, synchronization with other events, and regulatory constraints (typically 1 month or less).

**Related Concepts:**
- Sprint definition
- Sprint consistency (all Sprints same length)
- Risk management
- Business alignment

---

## Question 20 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Backlog Mutability

**Key Concept:** The Sprint Backlog is NOT fully formulated in Sprint Planning and DOES change during the Sprint as work emerges.

**Related Concepts:**
- Sprint Backlog nature
- Emergent work
- Sprint Goal vs. Sprint Backlog
- Self-management and adaptation

---

## Question 21 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Planning Timebox

**Key Concept:** Sprint Planning is timeboxed to a maximum of 8 hours for a one-month Sprint. For shorter Sprints, it's usually shorter.

**Related Concepts:**
- Timeboxes
- Sprint Planning purpose (3 questions: Why, What, How)
- Event scaling

---

## Question 22 Feedback

**Topic:** Scrum Team Size

**Key Concept:** A Scrum Team is typically 10 or fewer people. It's small enough to remain nimble and large enough to complete significant work.

**Related Concepts:**
- Scrum Team composition
- Cross-functional teams
- Scaling Scrum
- Team communication

## Question 23 Feedback

**Topic:** Increment Completion Requirements

**Key Concept:** Developers must complete enough work so that the Increment meets the Definition of Done.

**Related Concepts:**
- Definition of Done
- Increment quality
- Scrum Team accountability
- Done vs. undone work

## Question 24 Feedback

**Topic:** Scrum Terminology and Organizational Change

**Key Concept:** Changing Scrum terminology to fit existing organizational language may result in little actual organizational change and lost benefits of Scrum.

**Related Concepts:**
- Organizational change management
- Scrum adoption
- Empiricism and lean thinking
- Scrum framework integrity

---

## Question 25 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Backlog Ownership

**Key Concept:** No individual Developer can claim sole ownership of a Sprint Backlog item. The Developers collectively own the Sprint Backlog.

**Related Concepts:**
- Developer self-management
- Collective accountability
- Sprint Backlog management
- Cross-functional collaboration

---

## Question 26 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Timing and Continuity

**Key Concept:** A new Sprint starts immediately after the conclusion of the previous Sprint. There is no break between Sprints.

**Related Concepts:**
- Sprint as a timebox
- Scrum rhythm
- Event sequence
- Continuous improvement

---

## Question 27 Feedback

**Topic:** Scrum Framework Integrity and Adoption

**Key Concept:** Scrum should not be modified. It's a framework for dealing with complexity, not eliminating it. It must be implemented as prescribed.

**Related Concepts:**
- Scrum framework definition
- Scrum implementation
- All-or-nothing adoption
- Organizational resistance

---

## Question 28 Feedback

**Topic:** Sprint Cancellation

**Key Concept:** A Sprint can be cancelled only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete. ONLY the Product Owner has authority to cancel.

**Related Concepts:**
- Sprint Goal protection
- Product Owner authority
- Sprint commitment
- Risk management

---

## Question 29 Feedback

**Topic:** Process Improvement in Sprints

**Key Concept:** It's NOT mandatory to include process improvement items from the Retrospective in the Sprint Backlog.

**Related Concepts:**
- Sprint Retrospective
- Continuous improvement
- Team autonomy
- Process optimization

---

## Question 30 Feedback

**Topic:** Daily Scrum Consistency

**Key Concept:** The Daily Scrum is held at the same time and place each day to reduce complexity.

**Related Concepts:**
- Daily Scrum purpose
- Scrum rhythm
- Event consistency
- Team coordination
